The price tape for private equity secondaries

For years, selling a private equity stake early meant accepting a steep discount and a quiet process. That market has become the industry's pressure valve: record volume, tighter discounts, and pension funds — not distressed sellers — leading the supply. The pricing below is the closest thing private markets have to a tape, and it is read in percentages of net asset value, not dollars.

The strategy split is where the headline average misleads. Credit and infrastructure priced near par while venture sat near three-quarters of NAV and real estate lower still — the same market, very different verdicts on exit timing. Jefferies' own account of the shift matters here: LPs and GPs now use secondaries programmatically, on annual or semi-annual rhythms, rather than only when something has gone wrong. A market used that way prices liquidity as a service, and its discounts narrow when public markets cooperate and widen fast when they do not.

Methodology

Included: Jefferies volume and pricing figures (full-year 2024 and H1 2024), Setter Capital's H1 2024 survey as covered publicly, and strategy-level pricing compiled by Mercer Capital from Lazard and Evercore reports. Excluded: deal-level prices (not publicly disclosed), Evercore full-year 2024 volume (not verified this session), and Setter full-year totals (not verified this session). "Pricing as % of NAV" means the transaction price divided by the fund's reported net asset value at the reference date.

Disclaimer: Aggregated public estimates for information only, not investment or professional advice. Secondary pricing varies by fund quality, vintage, and structure.
